[Amadeus](https://amadeus.com/en) is one of the leading tech companies focused on providing solutions for air travel and logistics. They came to recruit Software Engineers to their Bangalore office.

The main two things you are going to hear about the company are they have a very good WLB going on, and that the projects they work on are usually not that challenging ( less opportunities for you to grow compared to a high growth sink or swim environment), although a few of their recruiters who came this year were talking about how the company is migrating to Cloud based infrastructure so you might have some interesting stuff to work on there.

Another thing that is commonly mentioned with the company is that they have a lot of CPP/Java development, but that shouldn’t be a problem since they weren’t screening candidates based on their knowledge of the above languages.

That all being said, let’s get into the placement procedure.

## RECRUITMENT CALL

We got their email detailing the Job Description and the CTC breakdown on 18th August, when the placement season started picking up.

Here is the eligibility criterion:

* 10th and 12th score must be 75% and above
* 7 CGPA and above currently in Engineering
* No current backlogs
* No diploma background candidates

The job offer came with a 5 month internship period from Jan/Feb 2023 with 30k/month stipend. Full time joining date was given as Jun/Jul 2023.

We got the date for the PPT which was to be held on 27th 2 days prior, but we couldn’t attend because there was a conflict with exams (KTU).

## INTERVIEW PROCESS

### Round 1: Technical Aptitude [ONLINE - VIDEO PROCTORED]

On 27th, at 3:00 PM we had our technical aptitude. It consisted of 29 multiple choice questions and 2 coding submission questions to be completed in 1:30 hours. It was conducted on Hackerearth. No negative marking.

The multiple choice questions were primarily in the format of code snippets of random languages such as Go, Ruby, Rust, C, CPP, Java, Python, JS etc. where we had to choose the correct output or the correct line of code to make the snippet execute.

The trick to solving these questions is to not get overwhelmed by the different languages, but instead treat all of it as pseudocode. A lot of inbuild functions across languages ( sort, lastIndex, etc.) are pretty self explanatory. You should be able to get how the logic of each snippet works and find the answer.

Another type of question I remember were the CS theory questions, though these were few and all over the place. A few OS questions (deadlock and stuff). Mostly the theory questions were from DSA. There were some related to Dijkstra and Floyd Warshal ( no implementation, just a high level understanding is enough ) and some related to Heap/Merge sort.

The first coding question was a basic array question about sorting/merging and creating a new array.

The second one was something like:

> “Given a graph with n nodes and e edges ( given as a nested list in the form of [[2,3],[2,4],[3,4]] ), find the least number of nodes to be removed so that the resulting graph becomes bipartite”.

Try to complete at least one coding submission as it has high weightage.

I would suggest studying just for the coding round instead of trying and preparing for the MCQs.

### Round 2: SHL (Logical Aptitude) [ONLINE]

Same day in the evening. It consisted of two rounds, Numerical Reasoning and Verbal Aptitude for a total of 1 hour.

The Numerical section focusses on quick calculation so be sure to have a calculator nearby. No negative marking for either.

### Round 3: Technical Interview + HR [ONLINE]

Interviews were scheduled for 3 days after. The interview was under an hour, conducted on Hackerearth platform. There was a technical interviewer as well as a HR interviewer present. Both of them introduced themselves, and the technical round began after asking me to introduce myself.

The interview consisted of DSA and DBMS questions primarily. For DSA I was asked about BSTs and how they worked, about traversal, application of BST and why we prefer it over array etc.

For DBMS, I was asked mostly basic and general questions about the structure of a database and how we create relations. One question I was asked was how are database queries executed faster as compared to searching through all entries each time a query is executed, and what data structure is used in this internal implementation.

Overall, the interviewer seemed more focused on checking if I understood the basic concepts in my own words rather than definitions being byhearted. They did not require that I knew all the specifics. Primarily focus on DSA and DBMS for this round. No SQL questions were asked.

For the coding question you will be provided with a code editor on the interview platform itself. I was given a basic question to iteratively print a pattern that was given by the interviewer. Only basic knowledge of loops, conditionals and input/output was checked. I was allowed to select the language of my choice.

A good practice is to ask the interviewer whether you should explain the algorithm or approach that you are taking before beginning to code.

After that, the HR interviewer took over and asked standard HR questions such as:

* “What do I define as success”
* “What is your strength/weakness”
* “Why did you choose Amadeus”

Research up on the company and prepare for the most usually asked questions and you should be fine here.

They also have a habit of asking puzzles, I got something similar to this: [Puzzle 27 | (Hourglasses Puzzle) GeeksforGeeks](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/puzzle-27-hourglasses-puzzle/). GFG has a good puzzle archive section, be sure to check it out for HR.

The final shortlist came after 3 days. 4 were selected for Full Time Role + Internship and one student was selected for Internship.

## Closing Notes

Compared to the previous year’s recruitment, ours was comparatively less intense. It would still help to be thorough with question patterns such as Greedy, Dynamic programming, hash map questions and array questions.

Give a lot of time on array questions, as they are the most common data structure asked on campus. That and an overall idea of CS concepts should be enough here.

Many students had issues with the online assessment platforms conflicting with browser extensions and antiviruses. Be sure to disable all of them before beginning your tests.
